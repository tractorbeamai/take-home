# Client Screen (Carlyle)

**Duration:** 30 minutes
**Interviewer:** Shane (The Carlyle Group)
**Scheduling:** Request availability from candidate, then propose 2-3 slots to Shane

## Interview Prep (Gem internal note — visible to Shane)

This is the final evaluation before an offer. Shane has a veto. The goal is to pressure-test whether this candidate can hold their own with the client team — technical credibility, composure, and communication under direct questioning. Shane should push hard. We want to surface any cracks before we make an offer, not after.

**Before scheduling, send Shane:**
- Candidate name and background summary (2-3 sentences)
- The role they'd fill and what projects they'd work on
- Anything specific to probe based on earlier interviews

## Timing Breakdown

| Time | Section |
|------|---------|
| 0-2 min | Shane introduces himself and the Carlyle team's work |
| 2-15 min | Shane digs into the candidate's technical background and relevant experience |
| 15-25 min | Pressure questions — challenge their thinking, test how they handle pushback |
| 25-30 min | Candidate questions about Carlyle's team and projects |

## Guidelines for Shane
- This person has already passed our technical and culture interviews. Your job is to stress-test them.
- Push on their answers. Ask follow-ups like "Why that approach?" "What would you do if that failed?" "Convince me."
- You're evaluating: Can I work with this person? Will they hold up when things get hard? Do I trust their judgment?
- Don't worry about selling Tractorbeam — that's our job. Focus on your assessment.

## Suggested Questions for Shane
- "Walk me through a technically complex problem you solved recently. I'm going to ask a lot of follow-ups."
- "If you joined this project on day one, what would you do in your first two weeks?"
- "I disagree with that approach — here's why. How would you respond?" *(deliberate pushback)*
- "Tell me about a time something went wrong on a project. What was your role in it?"
- "What's the hardest feedback you've received, and what did you do with it?"
